Role and Organization

HCA Healthcare is the largest for-profit hospital operator in the world. 190 hospitals. 2,400+ sites across 20 states and the United Kingdom. FY2025 revenue $75.6 billion. Net income $6.8 billion. 313,000 employees. Medicare and Medicaid account for 21.9% of total revenue (Medicare 15.3%, Medicaid 6.6%) per FY2024 10-K. Hazen has served on the FAH board since at least 2013. He served as FAH Chair in 2023.

Fraud and Legal History

The $1.7 Billion Settlement

The largest healthcare fraud settlement in U.S. history at the time. HCA (then Columbia/HCA) resolved criminal and civil allegations totaling over $1.7 billion between 2000 and 2003.

Criminal pleas: Two subsidiaries — Columbia Homecare Group Inc. and Columbia Management Companies Inc. — pleaded guilty to conspiracy to defraud the United States and to violate the Medicare Anti-Kickback Statute.

Specific charges documented:

  • Submitting false cost reports to Medicare
  • Billing Medicare for personnel at home health agencies and wound care centers
  • Billing for patients diagnosed with pneumonia
  • Paying physicians for referrals through free rent, office renovations, and loans with no expectation of repayment
  • Billing Medicare for non-reimbursable costs including Kentucky Derby tickets
  • Claiming pension contributions

Corporate Integrity Agreement: Eight-year CIA with HHS OIG, signed December 2000, concluded 2009. No active CIA as of 2026.

Sources: DOJ press release December 14, 2000; DOJ press release June 26, 2003

Mission Hospital — Immediate Jeopardy

Mission Hospital (Asheville, NC), an HCA facility, was placed under Immediate Jeopardy status by CMS in late 2023 and early 2024 due to systemic safety failures. As of February 3, 2026, CMS rejected the hospital’s latest plan of correction, citing “systemic and recurring patterns” of harm.

Source: AVL Watchdog, February 3, 2026

Service Eliminations

Research Medical Center (Kansas City), part of HCA Midwest Health, closed its neonatal intensive care unit in 2025.

Source: Staff Relief Inc., August 29, 2025

Private Equity History

November 2006: HCA taken private in a $33 billion leveraged buyout by KKR, Bain Capital, and Merrill Lynch, alongside the Frist family. Transaction included assumption of approximately $11.7 billion in existing debt. In 2010, PE owners extracted a $4.25 billion dividend recapitalization. HCA re-IPO’d in March 2011, raising approximately $3.79 billion.

Sources: HCA investor relations; PitchBook
